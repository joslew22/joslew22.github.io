Design Philosophy

Three principles I design by

01

Reduce the friction to show up

Most products don't fail on features. They fail at the moment someone hesitates. I look for the one step that makes people give up and remove it.

02

Design with the community, not for it

The best insights come from the people already living the problem: hoopers at the park, students in the dining hall, Reddit threads at 1 PM on a Sunday. I go where they are before I open a design tool.

03

Let the data earn its place

My engineering and quant background means I measure. Every data point on a screen should help someone decide something. If it doesn't, it's clutter.

It combines two independent signals, an LLM assessment (Groq Llama) and stylometric heuristics, into one confidence score. UX Case Study

Got Next: Know the run before you lace up

Pickup basketball runs on word of mouth. Got Next makes it visible, so players can find a good run, know how long the wait is, and show up with confidence.

RoleProduct Designer & Frontend Dev
PlatformMobile-first web app
FocusDiscovery & real-time court status
ToolsFigma, HTML/CSS/JS, CodePath WEB102

Overview

Pickup basketball is one of the most social sports there is, but finding a game is still guesswork. Players drive to a court and find it empty. Others find it packed with a 45-minute wait, or locked. The information they need, like who's playing, how many teams have next, and whether the run is competitive or casual, lives in group chats, Instagram stories, and the heads of the regulars.

Got Next is a pickup-basketball finder that brings that information into one place. Players can see nearby courts, check live activity and the "next" queue, and decide in seconds whether a run is worth the trip. As a player myself, I started this project from a frustration I deal with every week. My role covered the full arc: the initial concept, user research with fellow hoopers, information architecture, UI design, and building the frontend.

Problem Statement

Recreational basketball players don't have a reliable way to know which nearby courts have an active run, how long they'll wait, or what skill level to expect. As a result, they waste trips, wait in uncertainty, and often skip playing altogether.

How might we give hoopers a real-time picture of nearby runs so they can confidently decide where and when to play?

Who this is for

The Regular

Plays 3–4 times a week at the same courts. Knows the scene but loses time when their usual spot is dead or overcrowded.

The Newcomer

A student or transplant new to the city. Wants to play but doesn't know where the good runs are or whether they'll fit in skill-wise.

Key pain points

  • Wasted trips: courts are empty, closed, or full with no warning.
  • Invisible waits: there's no way to know how many teams "got next" before arriving.
  • Skill mismatch: players can't tell whether a run is casual or highly competitive.
  • Information is scattered: the info lives in private group chats and stories that newcomers can't reach.

Goals & success measures

  • User goal: decide where to play in under 30 seconds of opening the app.
  • Product goal: make court activity visible and trustworthy through lightweight check-ins.
  • Success signals: fewer reported wasted trips, repeat weekly check-ins, and newcomers finding a regular run.
